Michaela Klimova

Postdoctoral Research Associate, Duke University

michaela.klimova@duke.edu    

I study how neural gain control mechanisms shape the intensity and character of visual experience. Gain control — the brain's ongoing regulation of neural signal strength — is one of the most fundamental operations in sensory processing, implemented through divisive normalization. My work examines how these mechanisms operate in early visual cortex, how they adapt under altered conditions like dim lighting and sleep, and how they relate to subjective aspects of experience, including mental imagery vividness and visual discomfort.

I use functional MRI, computational modeling, psychophysics, and eye-tracking to approach these questions. I did my PhD at Boston University with Sam Ling, followed by postdoctoral work with MiYoung Kwon at Northeastern and my current position in the Subjectivity Lab with Jorge Morales at Duke.
